Why FIFA Music Matters

More than background noise

If you have ever spent hours building your Ultimate Team, completing SBCs, searching the transfer market, or waiting for the next match, you probably know how quickly FIFA’s music becomes familiar.

At first, these tracks may seem like background noise. For millions of players, however, they become connected to particular seasons, favorite teams, friendships, and periods of their lives. Repetition turned unfamiliar songs into memories. FIFA’s soundtracks were therefore more than decoration: they gave EA a powerful role in determining what millions of players heard and which artists they discovered.

The Historical Shift

From recognizable hits to intentional discovery

Between FIFA: Road to World Cup 98 and FIFA 14, EA changed both the sound and purpose of its soundtrack.

1997

Recognition

FIFA 98 relied on a small selection of licensed music remembered especially for Blur’s already-successful “Song 2.” Familiar music gave the game energy and mainstream credibility.

The Shift

Global Curation

EA built a research network that followed independent labels and local scenes, listened beyond radio charts, and tried to identify music before it became widely popular.

2013

Discovery

FIFA 14 mixed better-known acts with rising indie, EDM, hip-hop, and international performers, introducing local music to players across national borders.

Scholarly Conversation

How writers explain the “FIFA song”

Aditya Padinjat argues that the series gradually developed a recognizable style now known as a “FIFA song.” These tracks are energetic, memorable, and often selected before their performers achieve widespread popularity. His history traces the soundtrack’s movement away from stadium-focused rock toward a more varied combination of indie, electronic, hip-hop, and other genres.

Ravens N Riffs goes further by comparing FIFA to a music-discovery service before Spotify and other recommendation platforms became dominant. That comparison reveals the unusual cultural power of a videogame soundtrack: players did not need to search for every song. They encountered the music repeatedly through an activity they already enjoyed.

Alex Zets identifies fun, diversity, and exposure as defining characteristics of FIFA music. He also argues that the hours players spent in menu-heavy modes such as Ultimate Team increased the soundtrack’s influence. Together, these writers suggest that FIFA’s musical identity emerged through both careful selection and repeated listening.

Exhibit takeaway: FIFA’s transformation was not an accident. EA deliberately built a global curation system that could connect local music scenes and developing artists with an international audience of players.

What to notice as you explore

Watch how FIFA’s international distribution and repeated menu play broadened who could hear each song. Also consider who benefited: emerging artists gained access to new listeners, players discovered music outside their usual habits, and EA strengthened its identity as a cultural tastemaker.

The Larger Lesson

A sports game became a cultural middleman

By 2013, FIFA did more than accompany gameplay. It connected local music scenes, developing artists, and a worldwide population of players. Its history challenges the idea that videogame music is only background sound and demonstrates how repeated listening can shape taste, opportunity, and nostalgia.
